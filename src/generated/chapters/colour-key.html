B Color Key and Style Guide#

This appendix explains the visual conventions used throughout the survey for boxes, emphasis, and structural elements.

B.1 Box Types and Colors#

Definition boxes introduce core concepts and provide precise technical definitions. Use these when a term needs clear specification before use.

Example concepts: features, circuits, SAEs, privileged access hypothesis.

Text color: Dark blue border, light blue background.

Example boxes provide concrete illustrations, worked examples, or case studies. Use these to make abstract concepts concrete.

Example contents: “Feature vs. Neuron: An Illustration,” “MLP as Feature Combination,” worked examples of causal tracing.

Text color: Green border, light green background.

Insight boxes explain intuitions behind mathematical or conceptual results. Use these to build understanding of why something works, not just that it works.

Example contents: “Why Orthogonalization Works,” “Superposition in High Dimensions,” “Mechanistic Interpretation of Attention.”

Text color: Gold border, light gold background.

Warning boxes highlight critical limitations, failure modes, or common misconceptions. Use these to prevent readers from adopting incorrect or unsafe interpretations.

Example contents: “Steering Vector Brittleness,” “The Probe Causality Problem,” “The Explanation Validation Crisis.”

Text color: Red border, light red background.

B.2 Citation Evidence Levels#

This survey cites both peer-reviewed publications and preprints. To help readers assess evidence strength, citations are color-coded:

  • Orange citations: Peer-reviewed publications (conferences, journals)

  • Green citations: Preprints, technical reports, or emerging results awaiting peer review

Results supported primarily by green citations should be considered preliminary. We encourage readers to verify the current publication status of cited work.
